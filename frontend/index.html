The words on this page, defined once
bandThe one concentrated Uniswap position the hook holds, around the starting price. Its width is chosen at deployment (±10% here). This is the only place the queue applies.
wingsOrdinary Uniswap liquidity anyone may add on price ranges that do not overlap the band — through PositionManager, any router, no seat and no rent. They are the subordinated tail: reached only once the band is emptied.
seatOne of 32 numbered places in the band, an ERC‑6909 token of supply one. Buying a seat buys position in line, not liquidity — you still put your own capital in.
rankWhere your seat sits in the fill order. Rank 1 is served by every trade; rank 32 only by a trade that has already emptied 1–31.
self‑assessed priceThe number you post as what your seat is worth. Anyone may buy it from you at that number at any time.
rentτ = 10%/year of your own self-assessed price, charged continuously and paid backward, to the seats behind you. Not to a protocol, not to a treasury.
floatCapital the hook holds but has not yet deployed into the position (because the position can only take matched pairs of both tokens). It is still yours and still withdrawable.
markoutWhat a fill was worth shortly afterwards. Negative markout is the cost of having traded against someone better informed — the thing LPs actually lose to.
The book

Rank is an asset. It has a holder, a price, and a rent bill.

Each seat is an ERC‑6909 token, supply one, held under a self-assessed Harberger lease: you post your own price, you pay continuous rent on it to the seats behind you, and anyone may take it from you at your own number, at any time.

The only question that decides whether this is a product

Who buys which seat, and when they lose

The product is the seats. If no seat pays, there is no product. Here is the arithmetic per seat, with the losing condition written next to the winning one.

One caveat that governs every number here. The band is fixed for the life of the pool, and on a 45%-vol pair that life is ~16 days before the price leaves it. Every rate below is a rate while in band. QUEUE is a rolling fixed-term instrument — like rolling two-week paper, not a perpetual venue. You get the rate if you redeploy; ~1/20th of it if you don't.

Should seats hold the same capital? No — equal seats are the mistake that creates the trap. The tail's benchmark is the question the whole product turns on. Against an ordinary LP position it only wins in a toxic pool. Against tokens sitting idle in a treasury it wins every time — and that is the honest customer: capital that was never going to LP, paid a coupon to sit somewhere it also provides depth and takes almost none of the arbitrage loss.
The test, against a real pool, with no model of ours. Buy the tail when your pool loses more to arbitrageurs than ~89% of what it collects in fees. Stop above ~168% — past that the head stops paying rent and a plain wallet wins. Measure it from the pool's own history: mark inventory at t+5min on every swap, sum the signed P&L, divide by fees. A subgraph query. Stablecoin pairs: never. Volatile and long-tail pairs: common.

What is honestly wrong with this

  1. The pools where the tail pays are the pools rational LPs are already leaving. The addressable market is people who have concluded LPing there loses money and want the exposure anyway — and there are at most 31 of them, because the roster is capped.
  2. Routing viability and seat value are anti-correlated. QUEUE is routed only where it is the deepest venue — long-tail pairs — which are exactly the pairs with thin, adversely-selected flow. That, not the +119% gas, is the sharpest objection, and no engineering closes it.
  3. The tail's protection fails in the event it most cares about. Untouched by every move that does not reach it — and reaching it means the price is at the band edge. (The traversal costs the tail 0.25% of capital against a pro-rata LP's 2.42%, a tenth, but it is one event, not a stream.)
  4. Rent can never make the tail whole. Only θ = τ/(τ+k) = 29% of the head's advantage moves backward; the rest is capitalised into the seat price. θ→1 only as τ→∞. Rent is a discipline device, not the tail's income — the income is the avoided arbitrage loss.
  5. A QUEUE LP cannot re-mint around the price, which every ordinary v4 LP can. Fixed term. Roll it.

The objection the design lives or dies on

Why paid seats, and not an open queue?

The obvious design is an open, arrival-ordered queue: first to deposit is first in line, anyone may join the back, nobody pays anything. That is what "a queue" normally means. It dies on block one. Press the button.

Open queue — rank by arrival

Whoever deposits first is first. Joining is free.
Free rank is griefable rank. If arrival grants rank, the head of the book costs one wei. Dust it and you own the front forever for the price of gas — extracting the best fill on every trade that arrives.

QUEUE — bounded roster, always for sale

No runtime path creates a seat. Rank changes hands only by paying for it.
Scarce, but never capturable. Dusting buys nothing at any price — the roster was minted once, in the constructor. And a bounded roster alone would be a cartel, so the lease is what stops it: under-price your seat and you lose it.

Neither half survives alone. A bounded roster without Harberger is a cartel; Harberger without a bounded roster prices nothing — a non-scarce asset has no price, so no rent flows, and the tail is never paid for standing aside. Scarcity is not a gas concession made reluctantly. Scarcity is the mechanism.

What this is, stated precisely

Every market prices queue position. Most price it in time, which is burnt.

QUEUE is not price–time priority, and anyone who knows markets will say so in the first thirty seconds. Its roster is bounded and the "time" is gone. That is deliberate: on-chain, "arrival" is a gas auction, so time-priority reduces to a latency race whose spend is burnt on infrastructure and paid to nobody in the market.

So QUEUE deletes time and keeps price. What is scarce is the slot, not the participant — under an always-for-sale lease anyone may enter at any moment, at a price the incumbent posted themselves. The queue is not closed. It has a permanently posted entry price, and that price is paid to the LPs you are standing in front of.

Not an order book
Other side
An order book orders traders' orders — limit prices, matching, cancellation. QUEUE orders LPs' fills and has none of those. A trader never interacts with the queue: they swap through any ordinary router, same price, same fee.
Not an auction
Continuous
No rounds, no winner, no per-block reference anywhere in the contract. A self-assessed, always-for-sale lease with continuous rent.
Not am-AMM
Many holders
am-AMM auctions management rights over the whole pool to one winner per block. QUEUE sells an ordering over the existing LPs' own capital, perpetually, to many simultaneous holders.
Not toxicity detection
No classifier
Proven impossible for a v4 hook — every signal is forgeable by splitting a quantity, shopping addresses, or waiting one block. QUEUE classifies nobody; it sells slices of the flow and lets capital bid.
Not a margin engine
No oracle
Foreclosure demotes, never seizes: run the meter dry and you lose your place, not your money. No collateral, no mark, no liquidation crank.
Stated first, not under questioning

What it does not do

A judge who finds a limitation you did not mention discounts everything else. Here is the list at full strength.

Harberger cannot price a seat below zero

Under toxic flow every holder declares near zero, no rent flows, and the front is free to take. That is the mechanism behaving correctly, and it is also the point at which the price signal is censored. Unsolved, and not solvable inside this design.

A typical swap costs 119% more compute — not 119% more of the trade

Measured on the shipped configuration: 153,110 vs 69,970 on an identical pool with no hook. That is extra network work — not a worse price and not a higher trading fee. It is flat in roster depth, so it is a constant a trader can price rather than something that grows with the book. Walking the whole SHIPPED roster is a different bill: 620,668, at ~18,450 per seat walked. On an L2 this is cents; on mainnet it is a real bill. Aggregators may route around the pool for the gas alone — a real commercial risk.

32 seats — a professional venue, not every pool

32 desks can empty the book the way any thin market-maker list can pull quotes. They cannot seize it, pause it, or trap withdrawals — taking money out does not destroy the seat. What binds the number is depositing at 2,610,805 compute with 31 priced seats ahead (quadratic), not the walk, which is linear. 100 or 500 is a different program.

The paid desks cover ~±10%, not ±1%

One Uniswap range around today's price, shared by the 32 desks. Further out is ordinary Uniswap. A huge trade fills the desks first, then that ordinary liquidity. Anyone may move the desks to a new price if the ticks are empty. A ±1% specialist range would be deeper; this version uses ±10%.

Rent is currency0, weighted by currency0

Weighting a two-token basket needs a price and QUEUE is not allowed to have one. A tail holding only currency1 is paid nothing; the rent waits until an eligible recipient exists.

Face value is an upper bound

v4 does not agree with itself about rounding. Measured shortfall stays under one part per billion of everything ever deposited — and it is borne by the last holders to withdraw.

Enforcement needs somebody to poke it

No keeper ships and none is required — the seats behind are paid by the poke — but a seat nobody wants and nobody pokes accrues a debt nothing collects.

The founding roster is an endowment

Whoever deploys chooses the first holders. Every founding seat starts unpriced, and an unpriced seat is free for anyone to take — so the endowment is worth a head start of one transaction.
