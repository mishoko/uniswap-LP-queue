QUEUE.
Subordination for Uniswap liquidity — one LP takes the worse fills so another gets the better ones.
SIMULATED
A Uniswap v4 hook · not audited · testnet only

Someone has to be filled first.
Today nobody chooses. Now somebody can.

Uniswap fills every liquidity provider in a position pro-rata. Every trade takes a little from everybody, in proportion. That is not a setting — it is the only thing an AMM does.

QUEUE gives each funder a rank and fills them in order. And the part that surprises everyone, which we measured: being first is bad. The front absorbs every adverse move first, at the earliest and worst prices. So the front is a first-loss position, and everyone behind it does measurably better because it is there.

The one claim
Contract-enforced
subordination
One LP inside a single Uniswap position takes structurally worse fills so another gets structurally better ones. Enforced inside swap — not a payment step somebody can withhold, not a vote anyone can reverse.
What it is worth, honestly
$0
It is a transfer, not creation. The seats share one position, so the capital-weighted average return is the ordinary LP return. One seat above the line requires another below it, by exactly as much.
What it costs a trader
+119%
Extra network compute per swap: 153,110 gas against 69,970 with no hook, and 140,969 with the subordination dial turned off. No fee change and no worse price. On an L2 that is cents; on mainnet it is a real bill. QUEUE is an L2 product.

what a seat is worth

The tempting rule is wrong — in our favour.

   ✗ THE TEMPTING RULE      value  =  extra P&L  ÷  τ
   ✓ THE CORRECT RULE       value  =  extra P&L  ÷  (τ + k)

       τ = the rent, 10%/yr.   k = the buyer's own cost of capital, 24%.
       So you divide by 0.34 — not by 0.10.

   WHY THE FIRST ONE IS NOT MERELY IMPRECISE: dividing by the rent alone
   sets the price so that the rent exactly equals the extra P&L. The back
   seats then net zero, the sponsor pays nothing, and you have a dead
   equilibrium in which nobody gains and the product has no reason to
   exist. It overstates the fair ask by (τ+k)/τ = 3.4×, in our favour.

     the depth sweep moved TWO variables and credited ONE

Interactive · the identity, and the exchange rate

One point the front gives up. How much does the back get?

First, the rule nothing can break. All five seats fund one Uniswap position. So if you weight each seat's return by its share of the capital and add them up, you get exactly what an ordinary LP made. Always. Not approximately — measured to fourteen decimal places. 

   THE IDENTITY

     (seat 1's share of capital × its return)
   + (seat 2's share × its return)
   + …                                          =  the ordinary LP return

   So a seat above that line requires another seat below it, by exactly as much.
   There is no roster, no ordering rule and no premium rate that changes this.

Second, the exchange rate. If the front hands up one point of return, the seats behind do not gain one point each — they gain a share of one point, spread over more capital. The rate is fixed arithmetic, and it depends on one number only: how big the front seat is. 

   what the back gains  =  c₁ × (what a plain LP made − what the front made) ÷ (1 − c₁)

The application we lead with

Emissions-free liquidity incentives

Today: you print a token

  • You pay for liquidity by issuing your own token to LPs.
  • That dilutes every existing holder.
  • It creates permanent new sell pressure.
  • The capital leaves the day emissions stop.
  • And it is a governance promise — voteable down, changeable, quietly reducible.
Value is issued away and never comes back.

With QUEUE: you stand at the front

  • You post your own capital at rank 1 and accept the worse fills.
  • External LPs fund the back. They trade less than a plain LP — and a coupon paid forward out of your fees more than compensates them.
  • Nothing is printed. Nobody is diluted. You keep your capital — you simply earn less on it, on purpose.
  • It is not a promise. It is the order the contract fills people in, executed inside swap.
The cost is opportunity cost on money the treasury still owns.

Interactive · the rent, the price and the term

What a seat costs, and for how long you have it

A return quoted without its rent and its purchase price is not a return. There are exactly four numbers, and all of them are on-chain and visible before you buy.

1 · the price of the rank
Paid once
To the incumbent, at their posted self-price. You pay it to them, not to us. A seat nobody has ever priced asks zero.
2 · your capital
Not a cost
You still own it. The seat changes hands empty — the seller's money is returned to the seller, to the wei. You then fund the seat yourself, or it drops to the back for want of depth. Rank is backed by depth.
3 · rent
10%/yr, forward
Charged continuously on your own posted number, and paid to the seats ahead of you. You are buying protection from them; you pay them for it.
4 · the term
7 days
You may not voluntarily give up the rank for a week. You can always sell it, in any block, at your own posted price. A lock on rank, never on capital.

The rent cascade — who receives how much, from whom

Each seat's rent is split over the seats ahead of it, weighted by how much depth each of them contributed. Rank 1 has nobody ahead of it, so its own rent has no recipient and is simply held.

What is a seat worth?

A seat is worth what it earns you over a plain LP, divided by what holding it costs per year. And holding it costs two things, not one: the rent, and the return you wanted on the money you tied up buying it. 

   ✗ THE TEMPTING RULE, and it is self-defeating:   value = extra P&L ÷ τ
   ✓ THE CORRECT RULE:                              value = extra P&L ÷ (τ + k)

     k is the buyer's own cost of capital. At τ = 10% and k = 24%, that is
     ÷ 0.34 — not ÷ 0.10.

   WHY THE FIRST ONE IS WRONG AND NOT MERELY IMPRECISE: dividing by τ alone
   sets the price so that the rent EXACTLY equals the extra P&L. The back seats
   then net zero and the sponsor pays nothing — a dead equilibrium in which
   nobody gains anything and the product has no reason to exist. It overstates
   the fair ask by (τ+k)/τ = 3.4×, in our own favour.

The objection the design lives or dies on

Why paid seats, and not an open queue?

The obvious design is an open, arrival-ordered queue: first to deposit is first in line, anyone may join the back, nobody pays anything. That is what "a queue" normally means. It dies on block one. Press the button.

Open queue — rank by arrival

Whoever deposits first is first. Joining is free.

QUEUE — closed roster, always for sale

No path in the contract creates a seat. Rank changes hands only by paying for it.
Neither half survives alone. A closed roster without the always-for-sale lease is a cartel. The lease without a closed roster prices nothing — an asset anyone can mint has no price, so no rent flows, and the back is never paid for standing aside. Scarcity is not a concession made reluctantly. It is the mechanism.

Can a seat just be dropped the moment trouble arrives?

This is the question a subordinated instrument lives or dies on, and we found the hole ourselves rather than waiting for a reviewer. Three layers answer it.

Layer 1
Any payout costs the rank
You cannot be subordinate and liquid at the same time. Taking money out — any amount — sends the seat to the back.
Layer 2
You are takeable at your own number
You posted a price for the rank you just left. For an hour (FIRM_WINDOW) anyone may take it at that price.
Layer 3
The 7-day term
Inside it, a withdrawal that would demote you is refused by name. The one-wei dodge is not on the menu at all.
And what the term does not do, said by us rather than found by a reviewer: it does not stop a holder whose seven days have elapsed from stepping aside. Nothing mechanical can — no rule can claw back a loss the attacker never took. Layer 2 prices that case; layer 3 closes the free one. The test suite asserts both halves so that neither can be quoted alone. Full write-up: README.md §7.
Four things people assume it is

What it is not

Not an order book
Other side
An order book orders traders' orders — limit prices, matching, cancellation. QUEUE orders LPs' fills and has none of those. A trader never interacts with the queue: they swap through any ordinary router, same price, same fee.
Not an auction
Continuous
No rounds, no winner, no per-block anything. A self-assessed, always-for-sale lease with continuous rent — a property tax, not a bidding war.
Not toxic-flow detection
No classifier
Proven impossible for a v4 hook — every signal is forgeable by splitting a trade, using a fresh address, or waiting one block. QUEUE classifies nobody. It orders capital and lets capital choose where to stand.
Not a lending market
No seizure
Run your rent meter dry and you lose your place, not your money. There is no collateral, no oracle, no liquidation. Foreclosure demotes; it never seizes.

Stated first, not under questioning

What is honestly wrong with this

A reader who finds a limitation you did not mention discounts everything else. Here is the list at full strength.

It is a transfer, not creation. The capital-weighted average return is the plain LP return. Nobody is made better off in aggregate.

Simulation only. No live-pool data. 120 price paths per cell, one pair, one band width. Every per-seat number on this page is a simulated mean, and a seat holder lives one path, not an ensemble.

The front seat is a bad investment — on purpose. It is a first-loss position and it is meant to be held by somebody with a reason to volunteer, not by a yield-seeker. If no protocol has that reason, this is an ordinary Uniswap position with extra steps.

+119% gas for every trader. 153,110 against 69,970 with no hook. Aggregators may route around the pool for the gas alone. That is a real commercial risk and it is not hypothetical.

The sponsor must post real capital — roughly comparable to what it subsidises, locked in a fixed band that cannot be re-centred without withdrawing and redeploying. This is not leverage on a marketing budget.

The equilibrium seat prices are derived, not measured. They are arithmetic on top of measured returns, and they move with the buyer's cost of capital. What a seat actually fetches is set by the on-chain seat market.

A holder cannot be subordinate and liquid at the same time. Any withdrawal costs the rank. That is deliberate, and it is what makes a naive vault wrapper unsafe.
