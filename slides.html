what a seat costs

A price, a rent, and a term.

   ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

                              A seat nobody has ever priced asks ZERO.

   2  YOUR CAPITAL            not a cost — you still own it. The seat changes
                              hands EMPTY: the seller's money is returned to
                              the seller, to the wei. Maya funds the seat
                              herself, or it drops to the back for want of
                              depth. Rank is backed by depth.

   3  RENT   τ = 10%/yr       charged continuously on Maya's own posted
                              number, and paid FORWARD to the seats ahead of
                              her. She is buying protection from them, so
                              she pays them for it. Same way round as any

   4  THE TERM  = 7 days      she may not voluntarily give up the RANK for a
                              week. She may SELL it in any block, at her own
                              posted price. A lock on RANK. Never on CAPITAL.

what a seat is worth

The tempting rule is wrong — in our favour.

   ✗ THE TEMPTING RULE      value  =  extra P&L  ÷  τ
   ✓ THE CORRECT RULE       value  =  extra P&L  ÷  (τ + k)

       τ = the rent, 10%/yr.   k = the buyer's own cost of capital, 24%.
       So you divide by 0.34 — not by 0.10.

   WHY THE FIRST ONE IS NOT MERELY IMPRECISE: dividing by the rent alone
   sets the price so that the rent exactly equals the extra P&L. The back
   seats then net zero, the sponsor pays nothing, and you have a dead
   equilibrium in which nobody gains and the product has no reason to
   exist. It overstates the fair ask by (τ+k)/τ = 3.4×, in our favour.

how many seats · where we were wrong

We overturned our own headline.

   WHAT WE PUBLISHED:  "five seats is near the measured maximum"
   WHAT IS TRUE:        every back seat clears at every depth to 32

   THE DEFECT, and it is our own rule broken in our own experiment:

     the depth sweep moved TWO variables and credited ONE

        seats   N =  2      5      8     16     32
        head   c₁ = 66.7%  33.3%  22.2%  11.8%  6.1%   ← collapsing!
                                                    with N

     So "more seats stops working" was really "a shrinking head stops
     working" — and the head is the dominant parameter. The confound
     ran in the direction that flattered the shipped configuration,
     which is exactly why it survived review.

   RE-RUN WITH THE HEAD HELD FIXED AT 45%, both ladder shapes, 120 paths:
     the control reproduced four independent published numbers first,
     and then every back seat cleared at every N up to 32.

   ▶ We corrected it in our own documents rather than quietly dropping it.
